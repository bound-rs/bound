The Lighthouse Keeper

Every evening the keeper climbed the stairs of the lighthouse and lit the lamp.
The lamp turned all night, and ships far out at sea saw the light and kept away
from the rocks. The keeper wrote the weather in a book: the wind, the rain, the
fog that came in from the sea. Ships passed, and the keeper counted the ships.

One winter the fog did not lift for a week. The keeper kept the lamp burning
day and night, and rang the bell every hour, because in fog a light is not
enough. On the eighth day the wind turned, the fog lifted, and three ships
came safely into the harbour. The keeper wrote their names in the book.

When the keeper grew old, the lighthouse got an electric lamp that needed no
keeper at all. The book stayed on the shelf by the stairs, full of weather,
wind and ships.
